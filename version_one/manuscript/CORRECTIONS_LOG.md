## Part 1. Corrections to the mathematics

### C1 · S1 · The biphasic killing equation increases the population at its transition

**Version 1, Methods 2.1, Eq. 4:**
> N(t) = N₀e^(−k_fast·t) for t < t_c; N_d + (N₀ − N_d)e^(−k_slow(t−t_c)) for t ≥ t_c

**The problem.** At t = t_c the second branch evaluates to N_d + (N₀ − N_d)·e⁰ = N₀ exactly, for any parameter values, so the population returns to the inoculum at the transition regardless of first-phase killing.

**Computed.** Jump of ×2,981 (3.47 log₁₀) for *M. tuberculosis* at t_c = 80 h; ×403 (2.61 log₁₀) for *S. aureus* at t_c = 12 h; ×2.35 × 10¹⁷ (17.4 log₁₀) under the abstract's parameter set. `results/tables/eq4_discontinuity.csv`

**Correction.** Equation replaced by the biexponential N(t) = N₀[(1−f)e^(−k_fast·t) + f·e^(−k_slow·t)]. Version 2 §2.1 Eq. 4b, §3.1, Figure 2.

**Additional action required.** Version 1's Figure 2 shows no vertical rise at t_c, so it cannot have been generated by the equation printed in its Methods. Whatever code produced that figure must be located and the discrepancy disclosed. Version 2's Figure 2 plots the printed equation as printed, with the jump annotated.

### C2 · S1 · The same equation makes sterilisation impossible

**Version 1, Discussion 4.2** rests clinical conclusions on time to bacterial clearance.

**The problem.** lim(t→∞) N(t) = N_d > 0. The dormant subpopulation never declines.

**Computed.** With a 10⁶ CFU/mL inoculum and the reported 3.58% dormant fraction, a permanent floor at 3.58 × 10⁴ CFU/mL, which is 358 × the assay limit of detection. Time to sterilisation is infinite; the search window was extended to 10⁶ h before infinity was returned, so this is a property of the equation and not a truncated search. Both corrected forms give finite values: 1,290 h and 5,881 h for *M. tuberculosis*, 333 h and 161 h for *S. aureus*. `results/tables/model_endpoints.csv`

**Correction.** Version 2 §2.5 (endpoint definitions, with the search-window protocol stated), §3.1.

### C3 · S1 · The transition time is not a free parameter

**Version 1, Table 1 and Results 3.3** treat t_c as an independently fitted quantity.

**The problem.** In a two-subpopulation system the crossover is fixed by the other three parameters: t_c = ln((1−f)/f)/(k_fast − k_slow).

**Computed.** *M. tuberculosis*: 80 h asserted, **33.3 h implied**, a 2.41× discrepancy. *S. aureus*: 12 h asserted, **15.5 h implied**, 0.77×. `results/tables/tc_identifiability.csv`

**Consequence, and this is new.** The over-parameterisation is not merely inelegant. Profile likelihood on synthetic time-kill data at four realistic sampling designs finds the 95% interval for t_c reaching the edge of the searched range in **4 of 4 designs**, with the estimate on the optimiser bound in 3 of them. Jacobian condition number 5 × 10¹⁷ against 5 × 10² for the biexponential. **A fitted t_c from this equation records where the optimiser stopped, not a property of the organism.** `results/tables/profile_tc.csv`, `results/tables/fit_diagnostics.csv`

**Correction.** Version 2 §2.1 Eq. 4c (t_c as an output), §3.2, §3.8, Figures 2D, 6E, 6F.

### C4 · S1 · The resistance equation has no carrying capacity

**Version 1, Methods 2.1, Eq. 2:** N_R(t) = N₀e^((r−k_R)t)

**The problem.** Equation 1 declares a carrying capacity K which appears in none of Equations 2 to 4.

**Computed.** *S. aureus* at 240 h: **10⁵⁷ CFU/mL**, exceeding the estimated total prokaryotic population of Earth by 26 orders of magnitude, and 10⁴⁸ × the declared carrying capacity. `results/tables/eq2_unbounded_growth.csv`

**Correction.** dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K) − k_R·N, which retains a closed solution. Version 2 §2.1 Eq. 2b, §3.3, Figure 1.

---

### C5 · S1 · The tolerance equation omits replication, reversing its result

**Version 1, Methods 2.1, Eq. 3:** N_T(t) = N₀e^(−k_T·t)

**The problem.** No replication term, although Table 1 supplies a replication rate for both species. **Not identified in the original audit.**

**Computed.** *S. aureus*: r = 0.5/h, k_T = 0.2/h, so the net rate is **+0.30/h and the population grows to carrying capacity**. Equation 3 instead reports a decline of 15 log₁₀ over 240 h. For *M. tuberculosis* the sign is correct (r = 0.03 < k_T = 0.05) but the magnitude remains wrong. `results/tables/eq3_omits_replication.csv`

**Correction.** dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K) − k_T·N. Version 2 §2.1 Eq. 3b, §3.3, Figure 2D.

### C6 · S1 · The three strategies are the same equation

**Version 1** cites the Brauner framework as its backbone but writes resistance and tolerance as one exponential with different constants, and persistence as the same with a second phase.

**The problem.** Nothing in the mathematics distinguishes the three, so no measurement could.

**Correction.** A sigmoid maximum-effect function (Version 2 §2.2 Eq. 5) plus a two-compartment structure (§2.3 Eq. 6). Resistance is an EC50 shift, tolerance a joint slowing of replication and killing, persistence a subpopulation. Verified orthogonal:

| Variant | MIC (fold) | MDK₉₉ | MDK₉₉.₉₉ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wild type | 1.0 | 29 h | 152 h |
| Resistant (EC50 ×16) | **16.0** | 29 h | 152 h |
| Tolerant (metabolism ÷4) | 1.0 | **117 h** | 257 h |
| Persistent (dormancy ×10) | 1.0 | 32 h | **352 h** |

`results/tables/fig06_mic_mdk.csv`. Version 2 §2.4, §3.6, Figure 4.

**Note on the encoding of tolerance.** Dividing E_max alone shifts the MIC by about fold^(1/H), which is partial resistance rather than tolerance. Tolerance is replication and killing scaled together, which leaves the MIC exactly invariant. Version 2 §2.4 states this explicitly.

## Part 2. Corrections to the statistics and numerics

### C8 · S1 · The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test is inapplicable

**Version 1, Methods 2.3.3:** a two-sample KS test gave p < 0.05 between the species.

**The problem.** A two-sample KS test compares the empirical distributions of two random samples. Applied to two deterministic curves it tests whether the curves are identical, which is known before the test is run.

**Computed.** The p-value is controlled by the simulation grid: 5.2 × 10⁻² at 10 grid points, 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ at 25, 1.6 × 10⁻⁴¹ at 241, and underflowing to zero at 5,000, while the KS statistic itself moves by 0.04 across that whole range. `results/tables/ks_test_grid_dependence.csv`

**Correction.** **Deleted.** Version 2 reports no KS test. Figure S1A documents the grid dependence.

### C9 · S1 · R² > 0.9 does not support the conclusion drawn from it

**Version 1, Methods 2.3.3:** R² > 0.9 confirms a strong fit.

**Computed.** Fitting the printed equation and the biexponential to the same data at four designs: **both exceed 0.9 in 3 of 4 designs**, maximum difference between models 0.098, while corrected AIC differs by up to **84 units**. R² is compatible with a model that raises the population 3 log₁₀ at its transition. `results/tables/r2_vs_aicc_discrimination.csv`

**Correction.** R² is no longer used for model selection. Version 2 §2.7 reports corrected AIC, BIC, a residual runs test and the Jacobian condition number, with the reasoning in §3.8 and Figure S1C.

### C11 · S3 · The stated numerical methods describe a computation the paper does not contain

**Version 1, Methods 2.3.1:** SciPy `odeint` (LSODA) solved the differential equations; Euler at Δt = 0.5 h cross-validated the results.

**The problem.** Every model in version 1's Methods 2.1 is a closed-form algebraic solution. Nothing is integrated. Comparing Euler against an analytic formula measures Euler's truncation error, which at Δt = 0.5 h over 24 h is 11.4% and over 48 h is 21.5%.

**Correction.** Version 2 §2.3 states that Equations 1 to 4 are algebraic and require no solver, and that LSODA is used for Equations 6a and 6b, which are a genuine initial value problem. Figure S1B shows what the stated cross-validation actually measured.

### C12 · S1 · The sensitivity conclusions are algebraic identities

**Version 1, Methods 2.3.2, Results 3.3, Discussion 4.1:** *M. tuberculosis* persistence is most sensitive to k_slow, *S. aureus* to k_T; these findings underscore the biological relevance of biphasic killing rates.

**The problem.** For any t > t_c the second branch of Equation 4 contains k_slow and f and nothing else. The elasticities of k_fast and k_T on a 240 h endpoint are **exactly zero**, and k_slow necessarily ranks first, for any parameter values and any organism. `results/tables/analytic_elasticities.csv`

**Correction and replacement finding.** Version 2 §2.8 runs three analyses (OAT on the closed form, OAT on the structured model, and a variance-based global analysis with 36,864 evaluations per species). The replacement result: for the slow grower, **the first-order Sobol index for the resuscitation rate k_P→S is 0.780 (95% bootstrap interval 0.640-0.916), total-order 0.799 (0.769-0.830)**, with no other parameter reaching a first-order index of 0.08 and interactions accounting for 2.3% of the variance.

Additionally, **69.5% of the variance in time to LOD for the fast grower comes from parameter interactions**, where the resuscitation rate contributes exactly zero and the kill rate and replication rate reach total-order indices of 0.819 and 0.821 against first-order indices of 0.169 and 0.136. One-at-a-time analysis is the wrong instrument for that organism regardless of care. `results/tables/sensitivity_sobol.csv`, `results/tables/sensitivity_interaction_fraction.csv`. Version 2 §3.7, Figure 3.

This is worse than the earlier audit estimated. That pass flagged five entries as unlocatable; the full check finds thirteen entries requiring action, and identifies a specific failure pattern.

**Pattern: a genuine author string welded onto an invented title and venue.** Seven entries do this: 5, 11, 12, 13, 19, 22, 24. In each, the author list corresponds to a real paper, but the title, journal, volume or pages belong to nothing. These require full replacement, not field correction. Examples:

- **#11** — Nat Med 2018;24(8):1119 does not exist, and no paper carries the cited title. The named authors in that order match only Javid B et al., *PNAS* 2014;111(3):1132-7 (PMID 24395793), on mistranslation and rifampicin phenotypic resistance. **Whether that paper supports the sentence it is attached to must be checked, not assumed.**
- **#13** — Science 2007;317(5839):223-7 does not exist. The real paper is Koul A et al., *Nat Chem Biol* 2007;3(6):323-4 (PMID 17496888). The cited volume and pages are a mash-up with Andries K et al., *Science* **2005**;**307**(5707):223-7 (PMID 15591164), which may be the better citation depending on the claim.
- **#12** — real paper is Sebastian J et al., *Antimicrob Agents Chemother* 2017;61(2):e01343-16 (PMID 27895008), whose subject is de-novo *genetic resistance* from persistence-phase cells, not ATP depletion. The claim it was cited for is not the claim it supports.
- **#9** — authors and title exactly right, but the journal is *Nat Microbiol* 2016;1:16051 (PMID 27572649). The cited PLoS Pathog locus is an unrelated HIV-1 paper.
- **#22** — Nat Rev Microbiol 17:460 is a one-page erratum to reference 3, not a Windels paper. Real: *mBio* 2019;10(5):e02095-19 (PMID 31506315).

**Not found at all.** #14 (Zhang T et al., bedaquiline, JAC 2014) matches nothing; that JAC slot is a voriconazole paper. #20 (van den Berg van Saparoea et al., Biomed J 2019) returns zero hits on title, the volume has nothing at that page, and the named authors have no joint publications. It appears wholly fabricated.

**Wrong author on the framework citation.** #3, the Balaban 2019 consensus paper that supplies this work's operational definitions, is cited with the wrong title, the wrong issue, and **McKinney JD listed as an author when the fourth author is Ackermann M**. Correct: "Definitions and guidelines for research on antibiotic persistence", *Nat Rev Microbiol* 2019;17(7):441-448 (PMID 30980069).

**Cited for an unrelated claim.** #24 is a real paper on hydrogen-peroxide-mediated interspecies killing between *S. pneumoniae* and *S. aureus* (*J Bacteriol* 2006;188(13):4996-5001, PMID 16788209), cited in version 1's conclusion to support persister-targeting compounds. It must be dropped from that argument, not reformatted.

**Correction.** Entries 14 and 20 are removed. Entries 3, 5, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 19, 22 and 26 are replaced with their verified records. Entry 24 is removed from the argument it was attached to. Every remaining claim that rested on a replaced reference must be re-checked against what the real paper actually says, because in several cases the real subject differs from the cited title.

**New citations required by version 2**, all verified in full: Regoes RR et al. 2004 (PMID 15388418) for the Hill pharmacodynamic function; Nielsen EI and Friberg LE 2013 (PMID 23803529) for semi-mechanistic antibacterial PK/PD; Whitman WB et al. 1998 (PMID 9618454) for the prokaryotic-abundance bound in §3.3; and Tsuji 2012 (PMID 22526313), Drusano 2018 (PMID 29866864), Pasipanodya 2015 (PMID 26224767), Gumbo 2004 (PMID 15478070) and Conlon 2013 (PMID 24226776) for the datasets named as candidates for the fitting stage.

**This is the item most likely to end the paper if a reviewer finds it first, and it should be closed before anything else.** A bibliography in which thirteen of thirty entries are wrong, seven of them in a pattern characteristic of machine generation, will be read as evidence about how the whole manuscript was produced. No argument about model quality survives that.
