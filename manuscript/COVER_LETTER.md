I am submitting **"Score only the kill the assay can see: starting density and
the floor bound every MDK"** for consideration as a Research Article.

**What the paper shows.** The minimum duration for killing — the time to a 90,
99 or 99.99 per cent reduction — is the standard quantitative marker of
antibiotic tolerance. Because it is defined as a fraction of the starting count,
the inoculum is expected to cancel. It does in the definition. It stops
cancelling the moment killing reaches the lowest count the assay can report,
because from that point the recorded fraction is no longer a measurement of the
surviving population but the ratio of the assay floor to the starting density.

Two boundaries follow from the definitions alone, with no model and no fitted
parameter. For an assay floor *L*, a *q*-log endpoint is observable only in
cultures seeded above *L*·10^*q*; and above *L*/*c*₁, where *c*₁ is the lowest
class threshold, a floor-censored reading is compatible with only the lowest
tolerance class, whatever the drug did. Reaching the floor is the drug's doing;
the label it then permits is not.

Applied to five published time-kill deposits, this is not a small effect. In 217
clinical *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* isolates carrying their authors' rifampicin
tolerance classification, 33 could not have demonstrated the 99.99 per cent
reduction the classification is cut on before any drug was added — and all 33 are
recorded as failing to reach it. Under one written protocol, six laboratories
differed by 4.40 log10 in the kill depth they could demonstrate at all once
plated volume is counted. Held out entirely from the derivation, a hollow-fibre
experiment then behaves as the boundaries predict.

**Why it belongs in AAC.** This is not a new assay and not a new medium. It is a
quantitative account of what an existing, widely used antimicrobial killing
endpoint can and cannot measure, and of the conditions under which a published
tolerance call is interpretable. AAC's scope treats the validation of assay
methods and of their sensitivity, on a sufficiently large number of
determinations, as appropriate; the determinations here are 217 clinical isolates,
six independent laboratories under one protocol, and three further deposits. The
practical output is a calculation an investigator can run before seeding a
time-kill experiment — reported in Box 1 — that says which endpoints that
experiment will be able to report. I believe that is of direct use to AAC's
readership, who generate and interpret these curves.
