I am submitting **"The detection floor bounds what a time-kill assay can report:
minimum duration for killing and log-reduction endpoints in five published
deposits and a prospective test"** for consideration as a Research Article.

**What the paper establishes.** The minimum duration for killing — the time to a
90, 99 or 99.99 per cent reduction — is the standard quantitative marker of
antibiotic tolerance, and it is read off a plate count. Because it is defined as
a fraction of the starting count, the inoculum is expected to cancel. It does in
the definition. It stops cancelling the moment killing reaches the lowest count
the assay can report, because from that point the recorded fraction is no longer
a measurement of the surviving population but the ratio of the assay floor to
the starting density.

Two boundaries on the starting density follow from the definitions alone, with no
model and no fitted parameter. For an assay floor *L*, a *q*-log endpoint is
observable only in cultures seeded above *L*·10^*q*; and above *L*/*c*₁, where
*c*₁ is the lowest class threshold, a floor-censored reading is compatible with
only the lowest tolerance class, whatever the drug did. Reaching the floor is the
drug's doing; the label it then permits is not. Both are computed from quantities
a time-kill protocol already records — the day-zero count and the plated volume —
so neither asks a laboratory to measure anything new.

**Why this is a methods contribution rather than a critique.** The paper does not
introduce an assay; it establishes the measurable range of one that is already in
wide use, and gives a calculation an investigator can run *before* seeding, which
says which endpoints that experiment will be able to report at all. That
calculation is Box 1 and it takes three numbers. The consequence for reporting is
concrete: a tolerance call published without its starting density and its assay
floor cannot be interpreted by anyone else.

**The prospective test.** The boundaries were not only applied backwards. They
were used to design an experiment intended to break them — *Escherichia coli*
ATCC 25922 against ciprofloxacin at ten times MIC, seeded at three densities,
every sample plated at two volumes, with the predictions recorded before a plate
was counted. At the standard inoculum the deepest reduction the assay could
report ran 3.71 to 3.93 logs at the 10 µL plating and 4.88 to 5.05 at 100 µL in
the same three cultures. At a class threshold of 10⁻³, nine of 54 sample-times
received two different tolerance labels from one culture: one flask, one drug,
one moment, and the pipette decides.

**Scale of the effect in published data.** In 217 clinical *Mycobacterium
tuberculosis* isolates carrying their authors' rifampicin tolerance
classification, 33 could not have demonstrated the 99.99 per cent reduction the
classification is cut on before any drug was added — and all 33 are recorded as
failing to reach it. Under one written protocol, six laboratories differed by
4.40 log10 in the kill depth they could demonstrate at all once plated volume is
counted. A hollow-fibre deposit held out entirely from the derivation then
behaves as the boundaries predict.
